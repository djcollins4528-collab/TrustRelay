FOUNDING DESIGN PARTNER · PILOT CHARTER

One workflow. Sixty days. Measurable proof.

This charter is a practical working outline for a TrustRelay founding design-partner pilot. It is not a commercial services agreement and does not obligate either party to purchase or sell anything.

Objective

Test whether TrustRelay materially improves one recurring delegated-authority workflow by making the representative's scope and current authority easier for a relying organization to verify and audit.

Scope

  • One narrowly defined workflow.
  • One operational owner at the design partner.
  • Small internal test group.
  • 60-day evaluation period.
  • No-cost founder-led implementation support.
  • Institutional verifier portal first; Partner API only if the workflow earns the integration effort.

Baseline we capture

  • Approximate monthly authority-check volume.
  • Median handling or decision time.
  • Number of document requests/manual touches per case.
  • Escalation rate.
  • Common causes of ambiguity or rejection.
  • How revocations or scope changes are handled today.

What TrustRelay tests

  • Scoped grant creation and representative acceptance.
  • Signed/verifiable authority state.
  • Current scope and revocation visibility.
  • Institutional verification decision.
  • Audit evidence explaining the decision.

Success measures

  • Faster authority decisions.
  • Fewer repeated document requests.
  • Fewer manual touches and escalations.
  • Earlier identification of incomplete or over-broad authority.
  • Faster visibility of revocation/scope changes.
  • More complete verification audit records.

Data posture

Founding pilots should begin with synthetic, de-identified, or otherwise non-production test data unless a separate written data-handling scope has been approved. TrustRelay's paid commercial production activation remains fail-closed until its final external launch controls are complete.

Working cadence

  • Kickoff: map the current workflow and baseline.
  • Week 1: configure the pilot flow and test cases.
  • Weeks 2–6: run real-world simulations or approved pilot cases and review weekly.
  • Weeks 7–8: compare baseline vs. pilot outcomes and decide whether a production deployment is justified.

Commercial next step

If the pilot demonstrates measurable value, the parties can discuss a production deployment after TrustRelay's commercial launch controls are green. Standard lower-volume deployments may fit an existing organizational plan; enterprise, embedded, API, white-label, or higher-volume deployments are scoped separately.

What we need to start

  1. Name the authority workflow.
  2. Name the internal owner.
  3. Estimate monthly case volume.
  4. Estimate current handling time and escalation rate.
  5. Select a small test group.
